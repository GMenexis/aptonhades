# Description of the project

The machine is an SMC Mustang 50 quad, a very basic commuter for everyday, simple use. As with every great project, people tend to take a chassis not intended to do what people want it to do. And that's fascinating! And that was my plan, to make this 50cc commuter a missile. But that wasn't the plan from the start.

Originally, I did not know anything about engines, about the principles governing their operation. Well, I knew the basics, but nothing more that would make me believe that I would be capable of interfering with a two-stroke engine. However, the sad day came, and the engine bailed on me while riding the quad. One of the saddest days of my life became a significant landmark in my life. The seized 50cc engine allowed me to dismantle it step by step and encounter the inner components of the engine. That was the beginning of an inner fire that started inside of me and still goes strong. To this very day!

All of the above are not very significant as far as explaining the project's development, but their significance to me is of great meaning, because it was the start of everything important I could actually enjoy and be somewhat proud. Every first project is a major checkpoint in a builder's and engineer's life.


Enough with the above, it's time to talk with a sense of technicality. As mentioned before, we have an SMC Mustang 50, equipped with a Chinese Minarelli Horizontal engine, powered by the two-stroke cycle. This type of engine can be found in a large number of scooters, like the Yamaha Jog 50, the Yamaha Aerox, etc. In the beginning, I did not know the type of engine of the quad, which made it extremely difficult and stressful to find parts to fix the seized engine. The verification of the engine's identity was a crossover of me measuring parts from my engine and finding parts from the internet that matched exactly the silhouette of the cases and existing components that I had.

After knowing the engine's identity, I bought an Airsal Sport 70cc Cylinder kit, and after installation, I got my first victory, as the quad fired up after a significant amount of time. Due to the increase in displacement from stock 50cc to 70cc, the engine was much stronger from low to mid RPM, but at high RPM, the engine was choking. The reason for this behaviour was the stock old muffler designed by the factory to deliver low-end torque.

It is very important to mention that two-strokes rely heavily on the exhaust pipe to make power and burn the air-fuel mixture sufficiently. Because two-strokers operate with ports carved inside the cylinder walls, there is this thing called overlapping. That means that while the exhaust port opens, the intake port opens too, so the scavenging process and the filling of the cylinder with fresh air-fuel mixture happen simultaneously, thus providing a way for unburned mixture to escape from the exhaust port. This is very crucial in two-strokes, because efficiency drops heavily and power delivery is poor. 

That's why I went and bought one of the best pipes out there, the Yasuni Carrera 16 pipe, or C16. What an incredible exhaust pipe. When I first unboxed it, it gave me shivers just from looking at it. Anyway, originally, I could not install the pipe because there was not enough clearance between the engine and the chassis, so I made some space by cutting a big chunk of the chassis. I'm so disappointed by my actions back then, but mistakes happen, especially when someone is stressed and fed up with a part that will not fit. The fitment issues of the exhaust were known to me from before, because I bought a scooter exhaust to fit on an ATV. I know I'm problematic.

Anyway, I was able to fit the exhaust with the extra room created by cutting the chassis, and I enjoyed the quad for the summer. Although the setup was working, the tune was not spot on, and the parts could not support each other. The 70cc Airsal could never match the powerband of the high-revving pipe, resulting in a vehicle that was not that fast and not that enjoyable. Still a commuter.
